TCP congestion control prevents a sender from overwhelming the network by limiting the amount of unacknowledged data using a congestion window (cwnd), in addition to the receiver's advertised window (rwnd). The sender may transmit at most min(cwnd, rwnd) bytes. In slow start, cwnd begins at one MSS (or a small initial window) and increases by one MSS for every ACK, so it doubles every round-trip time (RTT), until it reaches the slow-start threshold (ssthresh). Congestion avoidance then begins: cwnd grows linearly, about one MSS per RTT (additive increase). A retransmission timeout (RTO) indicates severe congestion: ssthresh is set to half of the current cwnd, cwnd is reset to one MSS and the sender re-enters slow start. Three duplicate ACKs trigger fast retransmit: the missing segment is resent without waiting for the timeout. In fast recovery (TCP Reno), ssthresh is set to half of cwnd, cwnd is set to ssthresh plus three MSS, and the sender continues in congestion avoidance once a new ACK arrives. Together these form the AIMD (additive increase, multiplicative decrease) behaviour that gives TCP stability and fairness.
